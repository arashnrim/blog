It can feel easy to be swept up by the world today. For me, at least, I find myself living like I’m living life too passively sometimes — people my age are owning businesses raking in a staggering amount of revenue, exploring so many new interesting sidequests, or maybe even just sharing more about their own lives, and garnering a following on social media where they increasingly connect with more people who live and share similar life experiences with them. It feels overwhelmingly paralysing sometimes, the notion that, now more than ever, opportunities for you to become someone big are ever-bountiful by the moment. For me, it can feel sometimes like I just lack the bandwidth, the capacity, or maybe even the capability — the “it” factor — to do something worthwhile in my life.

Upon deeper reflection, this could honestly just well be my own version of a very early quarter-life crisis incoming.  And the adage that I find myself reflecting upon a lot nowadays — to push against the crowd; to create in a world of consumption — is something that I’d like to take a deeper look under the microscope. I think there’s a certain truth to that saying that could easily also become a trap for inaction and becoming overwhelmed by endless possibilities of pathways — choice paralysis.

# To create is to put yourself out there

There’s an ongoing surge of people my age, teenagers and young adults, creating accounts on social media where they share snippets of their lives. I know that this is essentially the point of social media, and people have been doing it since it was a thing, but this feels somehow different. I think, at the time of writing this, if I were to put my finger on what it could be, it could just be that these people are learning to curate an image of themselves: to create, selectively, carefully, meticulously, to project an image of a version of themselves. And, in many cases that I’ve seen, their efforts have paid off: many amass a following that continues to back them up, or maybe I’m confusing social capital for social worth.

But either way, there are ripples that come out of such actions, and I find it admirable in the confidence that people can have in themselves when they do decide to curate something of a name for themselves out there. Sometimes, I wonder if it takes confidence to create in a world of consumption, or whether it’s something that everyone naturally finds their own ebb and flow to. Sometimes, I question if I have it in me to even produce something — anything — worth sharing. But then again, you’re reading this, something that I’ve written, out here on the internet. And then it clicks in my head, something pretty obvious that doesn’t need to be said, but somehow has taken me a while to realise: that what you create doesn’t have to always look the same way as everyone else.

I can understand the appeal of creating on social media, and I can probably accept that doing that might also probably not be my thing. And that’s okay! What I make up for a reservation in actively creating through images and video on social media could be made up for with words that I write that reside in a blog out here in the interwebs. And one is not necessarily any worse than the other, I realise; there’s a certain reprieve to me with blogging that doesn’t come with content creation on social media, and I still feel good about writing words that, ironically, feel more emboldened with the knowledge that they might reach no one. We’re all creating, and that’s what clearly matters. And from the past few years of writing this little blog of my own, there have been amazing moments where some of my friends, both in real life and on the interwebs, have meaningfully engaged with it in a way I never expected and would always appreciate.

A social benefit to creation could just be that you become more sure of who you are. To create would be to unravel — or maybe, more aptly, unveil — more aspects of you regardless of the medium you create in. I used to think that I didn’t have what it takes to be like those cool people my age publishing their lives on social media. But I realised that this wasn’t a personal failing; merely a reframing that I preferred a different medium and that all it took was the things I already had — curiosity, determination, and continued interest.

# To create is to remember

I’d say a snarky part of this very early quarter-life crisis bit is the spirals you can go down that ultimately make you feel pretty useless, faceless, and worthless. It can feel pretty heavy thinking about how you’ve grown socially, especially in this part of your life — teenagehood and adolescence — and, perhaps wherein lies a lesson I still have yet to learn: the need for social performance and validation can feel stronger than the need to be sure of who you are. Somewhere in this picture, it can feel easy to forget everything you’ve amounted to, and I think there’s a case to argue that creating things helps you in this regard, because everything you created is a testament to your own abilities and effort. When you create something, it becomes an immutable fact that you’ve endured to produce something that inherently is of value regardless of whether you believe it does or not.

Holy cornball aside, I think this rings true especially to me. I can fall so deeply into self-comparison traps on social media, especially when coupled with those accounts that show a very curated version that screams “I’m living!”. My memory can also absolutely be the worst sometimes, and I can forget a lot about the things I’ve done that I’m proud of sharing. And then, in the moment of remembering the product, I suddenly remember what it felt like to be in the moment of creating — the agitation when something doesn’t work, the exhilaration when something unexpected does, the racing of the heart as my brain gushes out ideas when finding a problem. And with the trap of comparison, you could also, like me, be too blind to realise that you’ve made it unfair to yourself too: all the while, you’ve been moving and shifting the goalposts every time you’ve gotten something worthy to feel proud of!

So to create, in this sense, loops back to you because it becomes a memory of who you are. It’s a marker, a timestamp, that becomes proof that you’re capable of doing the very things that future you might feel that you’re not. And when you go back to look at the things you create and go, “huh, I didn’t realise I did things this way before”, it could even become something worth learning from and picking up from your past self too! A present from the past, who would’ve thought?

# To create is to grow

I was thinking of finding a great quote about creating, maybe something philosophical, life-altering, or meaningful. But I fell short, because honestly I don’t think that really falls in line with what I’ve written so far. And somewhere in this line of thinking, I realised I had a quick mental sparring match in my head; in fact, I wrote this sentence down:

> But I don’t think I’m anywhere near \[good at this craft of writing\] yet, but to create would mean to say that you’re definitely nowhere near the start, too.

At least you know I’ve 100% written all of my posts on this blog! But I digress; it’s honestly fascinating when your subconscious intervenes that way, and what I got out of this was somehow the idea that creating helps you to grow as a person. To create is to grow; I probably wouldn’t have stopped, paused, and intervened in my thinking, even subconsciously, if I hadn’t developed even an attempt at writing all those years ago. And quite honestly, if I were to look back at all I’ve written publicly over the last few years, I get to see just how much I’ve grown by using all my past writings as a stepping stone. You don’t have to be good at something to say that you’ve already made progress in it.

I’m wondering if this ties in to my initial concern, and the one that I originally wanted to explore more, about the “creating in a world of consumption” part. Perhaps I’m not advocating for creation as much as I am for rejecting passivity? Creating is a form through which you experience some level of growth within you; by writing this blog, for example, I became better at holding thoughts and transferring them down to pen and paper (or, more aptly here, keyboard and screen). This becomes evidence that dispels further unfounded doubts I had of myself — I used to think that I hadn’t grown much since I started this blog in 2022, but the fact that I’m reacting to them is saying something. Granted, it’s mostly cringe at some of the things I’ve written in the past, but to be cringe is to be free and allat, right?

I think there’s something distinctively human about creating. When you create, I feel like personal growth is guaranteed along with the process. Sure, some things feel like they haven’t contributed to your growth in any way — and honestly, they could well be those “surface-level” projects that you often do for some material reason, like grades or money — but there are those kinds of projects that you work on that make you discover more about yourself, who you are, and what you stand for. The food for the soul. Perhaps it could be pottery (something I’d love to check out one day!), and right now, for me, it could be coding something that solves a personal problem I’m facing, no matter how small.

And it’s quite funny just how unexpected that growth can be. Even writing this blog post, there’s so much I’ve come to stop and realise on my own. The many brain dump sessions I’ve had have increasingly become more sharpened, more to the point of what I wanted to convey. And maybe you might experience this for you, too, when you create something for yourself!

# But creating and consuming do not need to be opposites

I earlier made a mention of the adage to “create in a world of consumption”. That phrase implies that consumption is the norm, and, in the ways I’ve seen the phrase being used, somehow it’s also the very thing to reject and push against. But I’ve realised, as you may have read earlier, that a more apt point worth arguing might actually be to push back against passivity — the inaction, the doing of nothing — rather than simply consuming.

I feel like there isn’t anything inherently wrong with consumption. Together with creating, they can co-exist as a push and pull. In fact, I think it’s exhausting if one is always creating rather than consuming. I think that one can beget the other; people sometimes create things to be consumed by others (like social media content!), and sometimes consumption can inspire creation. One good example of the latter is all the builder/engineering kind of videos that I watch on YouTube — people like [Simone Giertz](http://www.youtube.com/@simonegiertz) and [Michael Reeves](https://www.youtube.com/@MichaelReeves) who inspire me in turn to want to use my coding skills for even wackier and more interesting projects.

So maybe I’m getting it wrong in my earlier drafts when I seemingly play to the tune of that adage. I think there’s some truth to it, but it almost presents this black-and-white picture that consumption exists as the antithesis of creation, and it’s what’s contributing to our brains rotting away. I think it’s worth taking a small step back and thinking more about this; what’s your own take on this?

# Continuing to create

I think there’s something pretty beautiful about realising a lot of things about yourself based around creating as you’re actively creating (i.e., me writing this very blog post). All the realisation I’ve come across thus far can become a good catalyst for even more deeper self-reflection and introspection for the path and times ahead. Relative to everything I have to experience in life, I’m still very early on my way, but it’s heartening to know that, as long as I continue to create, there’ll still be the opportunity to learn more about who I am and push back against the harsh bits of life that drag me down.

I think I like where my message ends for now and where the title of this post stands: that creating can really serve as a counter to your quarter-life crisis, namely because the very act of doing so brings things back to you as a person, an individual, a human on this earth. It’s quite a poetic take if you view creation as something so seemingly rudimentary, like I once believed it was. Maybe, more likely, it just isn’t that rudimentary after all, huh? ;)
